Chapter 1

The system at a glance

Occupy POS runs the whole floor: orders are rung up on the Register, cooked from the Kitchen screen, paid at the Counter, and recorded for the Admin dashboard. Every device shows the same live tabs.

The four areas

Register

Where orders are taken. Each customer or table has a tab. You add items, send food to the kitchen, print dockets, and send the bill to the counter.

Used by servers, baristas, managers

Counter

Where every bill is paid. Discounts, payments, splitting a bill, charging to a room, receipts, refunds, and the end-of-shift cash-up.

Used by counter staff, managers

Kitchen

A live screen of food tickets, oldest first, each with a timer. Cooks start them, tick off lines, and bump them when they go out.

Used by cooks and anyone with kitchen access

Admin dashboard

The back office: sales overview, all orders, the menu, promotions, stock and purchasing, staff, timecards, salaries, reports and settings.

Used by managers and owners

The pill buttons in the top bar move you between areas. You only see the ones your role allows.

The life of an order

RegisterOpen a tabPick a table or room, or make it a walk-in.
›
RegisterRing it upTap items. Add notes. Send food to the kitchen.
›
KitchenCook itThe ticket appears, is started, made and bumped.
›
RegisterCharge itThe bill is sent to the counter to be paid.
›
CounterPay itDiscount if needed, take payment, print the receipt.

Stock is taken off the shelf when the bill is paid, not when an item is added. A tab can stay open as long as the table is seated; more can be added to it at any time.

RememberThe register never takes money and never gives discounts. All payment and all discounts happen at the Counter.

Chapter 3

Signing in and your shift

Every screen sits behind a lock screen. You sign in with your own 4-digit staff PIN. Never share it: everything you ring up, discount or refund is recorded under your name.

Setting up a device (once)

The first time the system is opened on a new tablet or computer, it asks for the shop's account email and password (Set up this register). A manager or owner does this once per device. After that, staff only ever see the PIN screen.

Signing in

  1. On the lock screen, enter your 4-digit PIN.
  2. If you aren't clocked in yet, you're offered a one-tap clock-in. Take it at the start of your shift; this is what your timecard records.
  3. You land on your home screen (see Chapter 2).

Clocking out and signing out

Tap your name in the top bar to open your account menu. From there you can Clock out at the end of your shift, or sign out so the next person can sign in. Open tabs are not affected by signing out: they stay open for everyone.

Security features

  • Auto-lock: after a set time with no taps, the screen locks itself. Open tabs carry on. Sign in again to continue.
  • Wrong PINs: after several wrong PINs in a row, sign-in locks for 30 seconds.
  • Forgot to clock out? Tell a manager. They correct it in Admin → Timecards.
ImportantAlways sign out (or let the screen lock) before walking away from a shared register. Anything done while you're signed in is recorded as yours.

Finding items

  • Categories run along the top. Some open a second row (Drinks → Sodas, Wines).
  • Search finds any item by name.
  • Running promotions (happy hour and so on) show as chips under the categories, so you know why a price has dropped. They apply automatically.
  • Tiles warn about stock: "3 left" when running low, "Sold out" when there's none, "All on this tab" when the last ones are already on your tab.

Ringing up a tab

  1. Start a tab. The register always shows one. Use + New tab for a new customer.
  2. Assign it. Tap Assign table * on the gold tab card and pick a table or room, or tap Walk-in for a customer at the counter. A tab must be assigned before it can be charged or sent to the kitchen.
  3. Add items. Tap a tile to add one; tap again for more. The total updates as you go.
  4. Add notes. Tap an item's name on the tab to add a note ("no onions"). It prints on the kitchen ticket and the receipt.
  5. Send food to the kitchen with Send 2 to kitchen. The button only shows when there are kitchen items not yet sent.
  6. Charge it when the customer is ready to pay (see "Charging a tab" below).

Items that ask a question

  • Portions: some items come in servings (a half, a glass, a 25 ml shot). You'll be asked which.
  • Add-ons: categories can offer sides and extras. Pick them in the pop-up; some are free, some have a price.
  • Price at the counter: items like "market fish" ask you to type the price each time.
  • Custom amount: for something not on the menu, ring up a one-off amount.
  • Set meals: under the Set meals shelf. One price for several courses: pick what the guest wants from each course. Any course can be skipped, and the meal goes on the tab as a single line.

Working with several tabs

  • Open tabs ▾ (top of the tab) lists every running tab with its count. Tap one to bring it onto the register.
  • The Tables screen shows the floor at a glance: which tables and rooms have a tab running, plus walk-in tabs. Tap any to put it on the register.
  • Assigning a tab to a table that already has one merges them into a single tab.
  • When you move to another tab, what you rang up on the last one is saved to it. Reopen it any time to add more.

Dockets and bills

Two links sit just above the charge button:

  • Print bill prints the whole tab so far: every item, the subtotal, tax and total, with any discount and part-payments. Hand it to the table so they can check it.
  • Print docket prints only what's been added since the last docket, as either an Order docket (with prices and the total) or a Kitchen docket (items and notes only). After printing, those items join the rest of the tab, so the next docket starts fresh.

Charging a tab

The big button at the bottom says Charge to Table 4 (or Send bill to counter for a walk-in). Tapping it, or pressing Enter:

  1. prints an order docket of anything new since the last one,
  2. sends the bill to the Counter, marked Bill at counter,
  3. moves the register on to a fresh tab for the next customer.

The customer then pays at the Counter. The tab stays open, and can still be added to, until it's paid.

Taking items off a tab

The rule You can only take items off a new tab that you started, before anything on it has been printed or charged, and before you've moved away from it. While that's true, the − on each line, the ×, and Clear tab are shown.

Once a tab has been docketed, charged, or left and reopened, those buttons disappear for everyone, managers and owners included. You can still add to it. If something on an existing tab is wrong, tell a manager.

Items taken off after they were sent to the kitchen send a void ticket, so the kitchen stops making them.

Chapter 5

The Counter

The Counter is where money is taken. Its top bar has four views: Bills, Rooms, Receipts and Cash-up.

Bills: collecting payment

Bills to collect lists every open bill: the ones sent over from the register first, then the longest-running. Tap one to see it.

  • Discount the bill: take a percentage or an amount off the whole bill. This must be done before any payment is taken.
  • Charge to a room: put the bill on a guest room, to be paid at checkout.
  • Take payment opens the payment screen.
Part-paid billsOnce part of a bill has been paid, it can only be paid off: no new discounts and no room charges.

The payment screen

  1. Tip (if your shop uses tips): pick a percentage, a custom amount, or No tip.
  2. Method: Card, Cash, or Mobile money.
  3. Splitting the bill (optional): tap ÷ 2, ÷ 3 or ÷ 4 for even shares, By item to tick the items this person is paying for, or type an amount in This payment. Take each share as its own payment.
  4. Cash: enter the cash received, or tap a quick-cash amount. The screen shows the Change due (or how much the customer is short).
  5. Confirm. When the last payment covers the bill, it's Paid, and the receipt prints (or tap Print receipt).

A payment taken by mistake can be taken back from the list of payments, as long as the bill isn't finished.

Rooms

Room bills lists rooms with charges waiting. When a guest checks out, pick the room, see everything charged to it, and settle the whole room bill in one payment.

Receipts and refunds

The Receipts view lists paid orders. Pick one to Reprint receipt or to refund it.

  1. Tap Refund…. Everything is selected; untick or lower anything not being refunded.
  2. Choose a reason, and whether items go back into stock.
  3. Tap Refund. If your role can't refund, the button reads Approve: a manager or owner enters their PIN to approve it.

Cash-up: closing the shift

The Cash-up view shows This shift: money taken and refunded by each method since the last cash-up.

  1. Count the cash in the drawer and enter it under Cash drawer.
  2. The system compares it with what's expected, and shows whether the drawer is short or over, and by how much.
  3. Confirm to close the shift. The cash-up is kept under Past cash-ups, where it can be printed.
Good practiceDo the cash-up at every shift change, not just at closing. A short drawer is much easier to explain when it covers a few hours.

Chapter 6

The Kitchen screen

Tickets arrive from the register as soon as food is sent, oldest first, each with a timer running since it was sent.

  1. Start a ticket when you pick it up, so the floor can see it's being made. Tapped Start by mistake? Tap it again.
  2. Tap each line to cross it off as it's made.
  3. Bump the ticket once it's gone out. It leaves the screen.

What the ticket tells you

  • The tab name (table, room or walk-in) and order number.
  • Each item with its quantity, sides, set-meal picks and notes.
  • A second order for the same tab is marked as its next fire.
  • Late: a ticket waiting longer than the set time (Settings → Flag tickets late after) is marked late.

Void tickets

When food that was already sent is taken off a tab, a Void, stop making ticket appears, showing the quantities with a minus sign. Stop making those items, then tap Got it, clear.

Done: recalling a ticket

The Done view keeps recently bumped tickets. If one was bumped by mistake, recall it to put it back up.

When there's nothing waiting, the screen reads All clear.

Chapter 7

Order history

The Orders pill on the register's top bar opens the counter's quick lookup of every order.

  • Search by tab, item or amount.
  • Narrow by date range (today, this week, all time), status (open, paid, refunded and so on), table or room, and payment method.
  • Tap any order to see its detail.

What you can do from an order

Order is…You can

Servers only see their own open tabs here; everyone else sees all of them.

Chapter 8 · Managers and owners

The Admin dashboard

Open it with the Admin pill. The sidebar shows only the pages your role can use.

Overview

A one-screen summary: net sales for the chosen range at the top, round shortcut buttons for the nightly jobs (Register, Cash up, Add expense, Reports), the open tabs, sales by day, an at-a-glance panel, top items, and recent activity.

Orders

The back-office ledger of every order: open, paid and refunded. It has sorting, totals, longer date ranges, and Export CSV of exactly what's filtered. From an order you can reprint, refund, edit or delete a closed order (with a note on what changed and why), or delete an open tab. Every edit and deletion is logged with who did it.

Menu

Items & catalog

Every item, by category, with a restock view of tracked items that are low or sold out. Tap an item to open the item editor:

  • Name, price, category and tile colour, with a live preview of the register tile.
  • Available for sale: switch off to hide it from the register without deleting it.
  • Price set at the counter: asks for the price each time it's rung up.
  • Track stock: count units on the shelf, with an optional low-stock warning level.
  • Portions: sell part of a unit, either as a share ("1/2") or in millilitres for drinks (a 25 ml shot from a 750 ml bottle).
  • Recipe: for cocktails and the like. Each serving pours its ingredients from stock, and its cost is worked out from them.

Categories

The shelves along the top of the register and the headings in reports. Categories can sit inside others (Sodas inside Drinks). Each can offer add-ons: sides and extras, free or priced. A category can only be deleted once it's empty.

Set meals

Several courses at one price. Each course lists the items to choose from; at the register, any number can be picked from each course, or a course skipped.

Promotions

Automatic discounts such as happy hour, set to run on chosen days and hours. The register applies them as items are rung up; there's nothing for staff to enter.

Pick any date range. Every report is a table first, with charts over the same numbers, and Export CSV downloads the table on screen. Reports can also be printed on the shop's letterhead.

Expenses

Running costs that aren't stock or wages: rent, power, transport. Record them here; they come off gross profit in the Profit & loss report.

Stock and purchasing

  • Stock: what's on the shelf, its value at cost, and every change to it. Sales and refunds record themselves; anything else (breakage, spoilage) is an adjustment here, with a reason.
  • Stock count: walk the shelves and enter what's actually there, for everything, one category, or one supplier. Items left blank are skipped, not zeroed. Posting the count records the differences as gains or losses. An unfinished count stays in progress if you leave.
  • Purchases: purchase orders from draft to delivery. Create a draft, place it with the supplier (print it on the letterhead), then receive it, in full or in parts. Receiving puts the stock on the shelf, updates item costs, and raises a supplier bill. A delivery that will never arrive in full can be closed short.
  • Suppliers: contacts, payment terms, and where each account stands. Suppliers are deactivated, not deleted, so their history keeps its name.
  • Supplier bills: what the business owes, with due dates ("3 days overdue"). Only owners record payments against them.

People

  • Staff (owners): add people, set their main and extra roles, PIN and monthly salary. Deactivating keeps someone on the list but stops them signing in. Deleting removes them, though past orders still show their name. The page also lists exactly what each role can do.
  • Timecards: who's working now, hours per person, and the shift log. Fix forgotten clock-outs here. Timecards record hours only; pay is a monthly salary.
  • Salaries (owners): what the team is owed for a month. Pay a month whole, or in two halves (1st–15th, then 16th–end). Marking a month paid freezes it into the history.

Setup

  • Locations: the tables and rooms tabs can be assigned to. Changes show straight away on the Tables screen and the register's picker.
  • Settings (owners). Changes are collected and applied together when you tap Save:
    • Business: name, logo, location and register name (printed on receipts), receipt footer message, and whether to print automatically after payment.
    • Money: currency, buying currency and exchange rate, VAT/sales tax, tips on or off and the tip options offered.
    • Register and kitchen: price at the counter, the low-stock warning level, when kitchen tickets count as late, and whether cooks may manage items.
    • Security: auto-lock time and how many wrong PINs before lockout.
    • This device: sign out to remove the shop's data from the browser. It stays safe in the cloud and comes back on signing in.

Chapter 9

A day at a glance

A simple routine that keeps the numbers right.

Opening

  • Sign in with your PIN and clock in.
  • Check the receipt printer has paper.
  • Counter: check the cash float in the drawer.
  • Kitchen: open the Kitchen screen and check it reads All clear.
  • Managers: check Items & catalog → restock for anything low or sold out, and switch off items you can't serve.

During service

  • Assign every tab to a table, room or walk-in.
  • Send food to the kitchen as soon as it's ordered.
  • Add notes for allergies and changes.
  • Charge tabs when guests ask for the bill.
  • Never leave a register signed in and unattended.

Shift change

  • Counter: do a cash-up and hand over the drawer.
  • Make sure your tabs are charged or handed over.
  • Clock out and sign out.

Closing

  • Check Open tabs and Bills to collect: nothing should be left unpaid.
  • Counter: final cash-up; explain any short or over.
  • Kitchen: bump any remaining tickets.
  • Managers: record the day's expenses, and glance at the Overview.
  • Everyone: clock out.

Chapter 10

Questions and fixes

Common situations, and what to do.

I can't charge the tab or send it to the kitchen.It hasn't been assigned. Tap Assign table * on the tab card and pick a table or room, or tap Walk-in.
The − and × buttons have disappeared from a tab.The tab isn't new any more: it was docketed, charged, or reopened, or someone else started it. You can still add items. Ask a manager to sort out anything that needs to come off.
I added the wrong item to a brand-new tab.As long as it's your tab and you haven't printed, charged or left it, tap − or × on the line, or Clear tab.
A tile says "Sold out" but we have stock.The stock count in the system is wrong. A manager can correct it in Admin → Stock with an adjustment, or with a stock count.
I can't find a tab in Open tabs.Servers only see the tabs they opened. If it's someone else's, ask them or a manager. Bills already sent to the counter also show on the Counter's Bills view.
The customer wants a discount.Discounts are only given at the Counter, before any payment is taken. Send the bill to the counter and ask counter staff.
The customer wants to pay in parts.On the payment screen, use ÷ 2, ÷ 3, ÷ 4, By item, or type an amount, and take each part as its own payment.
A guest staying in a room wants to put a meal on their room.At the Counter, open the bill and use Charge to the room. It's paid when the room settles up at checkout.
I bumped a kitchen ticket by mistake.Open the Kitchen screen's Done view and recall it.
The receipt didn't print.Check the printer has paper and is on, then reprint it from the Counter's Receipts view or Order history.
I forgot to clock out.Tell a manager; they'll correct your shift in Admin → Timecards.
The screen locked while I was working.That's auto-lock. Sign in again with your PIN: open tabs are exactly as you left them.
My PIN won't work and now it says to wait.Too many wrong PINs locks sign-in for 30 seconds. Wait, then try again carefully. If you've forgotten your PIN, an owner can set a new one in Admin → Staff.

Chapter 11

Glossary

The words the system uses, and what they mean.

Tab
A running order for one customer, table or room. It stays open until it's paid.
Walk-in
A tab for a customer who isn't at a table or room, such as a takeaway at the counter.
New tab
A tab you've just started and haven't printed, charged or left. Only a new tab can have items taken off.
Assign
Attach a tab to a table, room, or walk-in. Required before charging or sending to the kitchen.
Send to kitchen
Fire the tab's unsent food to the Kitchen screen as a ticket.
Ticket
One order on the Kitchen screen. A void ticket tells the kitchen to stop making something.
Bump
Clear a finished ticket off the Kitchen screen.
Docket
A printout of what's been added since the last docket: an order docket (with prices) or a kitchen docket (items and notes).
Bill
The whole tab, priced, to be paid. "Print bill" prints it; "Charge" sends it to the counter.
Bill at counter
A tab that has been charged and is waiting to be paid at the Counter.
Room charge
A bill put on a guest room, paid when the room settles up at checkout.
Tender
Taking payment: the payment screen.
Cash-up
Counting the drawer at the end of a shift and comparing it with what the system expects.
Short / over
The drawer has less / more cash than expected.
Portion
A serving smaller than a whole unit, like half a cake or a 25 ml shot.
Add-on
A side or extra offered with items in a category.
Set meal
Several courses sold at one price, with choices in each course.
Promotion
A discount that applies itself automatically at set days and times.
Stock count
Counting what's actually on the shelf and recording the differences.
Purchase order
An order to a supplier, tracked from draft to delivered.
Supplier bill
What the business owes a supplier for a delivery.
Timecard
Your record of clock-ins and clock-outs.
PIN
Your private 4-digit code for signing in and approving actions.
Still stuck?Ask your manager. They can see every tab and order, fix timecards, and correct stock, and they know how your shop has set the system up.
